Let’s be real, for years, the programmatic playbook was simple: more paths, more scale, more reach. But in 2026, that approach is quietly costing advertisers more than they realise.

Here’s the uncomfortable truth: only 41% of programmatic ad spend resulted in quality impressions, meaning nearly 60% of spend was lost to non-quality inventory. That’s not a rounding error. That’s a structural problem.

So why are so many supply path strategies still built the old way?

## The Problem With Chasing Scale

-   More supply paths sound like more opportunity, but in reality, they mean more middlemen, more fees, and less of your budget actually working
-   Only about [70 cents](https://www.iabuk.com/member-directory/voisetech) of every dollar reaches the publisher in non-optimised paths, the rest disappears into the supply chain
-   When buying strategies run on autopilot, they almost always drift toward whatever’s cheapest, not whatever performs best

## What Supply Path Optimisation Actually Looks Like in 2026

-   [SPO in 2026](https://www.infor.com/blog/supply-chain-transformation-trends-2026) is no longer just about cutting partners; it’s about choosing the right ones.
-   Fewer paths. Cleaner supply. More of your budget is actually working.
-   Curation is now the sharpest SPO tool in the box, hand-pick your inventory instead of bidding blind.

## Where Curation Changes the Game

-   Pre-curated deals cut out the middlemen before the auction even starts.
-   Only the highest-quality inventory makes it through, no unnecessary third parties, no compromises.
-   Brand safety, audience precision, and transparency are built in, not bolted on.

## The Bottom Line

Scale without quality is just noise at a higher price point. The most [successful media buyers in 2026](https://affinco.com/media-buying-statistics/#:~:text=Media%20buying%20has%20entered%20a,%2C%20and%20mobile%2Dfirst%20campaigns.) aren’t the ones with the most supply partners; they’re the ones who know exactly which paths to trust.

Efficiency cannot be assumed; it must be engineered.
