Agent-to-Agent (A2A) communication is quickly becoming the next big shift in AdTech. Instead of relying on manual handoffs, buyer and seller agents can communicate, negotiate, and make decisions in real time.

It sounds like the perfect solution, but like any emerging technology, A2A isn’t without challenges.

The real question isn’t _whether_ A2A works. It’s whether your infrastructure is ready for it.

## Where A2A Systems Actually Fail

-   **Trust doesn’t happen overnight:** Businesses are still cautious about handing decision-makers over to [AI agents](https://tensorops.ai/labs/research/agentic-ai-advertising-2026-field-guide). Without transparency, it’s difficult to trust the outcomes.
-   **Real-world complexity exposes weak systems:** Recent industry analysis found that most high-profile [agentic AI](https://callsphere.ai/blog/ai-agent-failures-biggest-agentic-ai-disasters-early-2026) failures didn’t happen because the agents were poorly built; they happened because the systems were deployed without understanding the gap between demo-ready and production-ready.
-   **Agents need a common language:** If buyer and seller agents interpret data differently, even small inconsistencies can lead to poor decisions and inaccurate outcomes.
-   **Autonomy needs guardrails:** [Experts](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21012) point out that AI agents can be hijacked through indirect, hidden instructions buried in the systems they interact with, a reminder that agentic systems need real safeguards, not just good intentions.
-   **Technology isn’t the only challenge:** [Research](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/why-most-agencies-will-botch-agentic-ai-contrarian-2026) shows that internal processes, slow approvals, and organisational resistance often delay adoption more than the technology itself.

## How to Get A2A Right

Successful A2A systems are built on a few key principles:

-   Shared standards so agents understand each other.
-   Transparent decision-making that can be traced and explained.
-   Human oversight for strategic decisions.
-   Built-in safeguards that handle unexpected scenarios without disrupting performance.
