For years, digital advertising has worked on a simple principle: **if this happens, do that**. If an audience meets certain criteria, show an advert. If performance drops, adjust the budget. If a placement underperforms, reduce its priority. This approach made advertising more predictable, but today’s media environment is moving too quickly for fixed instructions to keep up.

The shift is already visible. Global advertising investment surpassed **$1 trillion in 2025**, while [dentsu](https://www.dentsu.com/news-releases/ad-spend-growth-is-projected-to-slow-to-5-percent-in-2026-still-outpacing-economic-growth) expects it to grow another **5.0% in 2026**. More than four-fifths of digital investment is expected to be programmatic this year.

## From Fixed Rules to Continuous Decisions

Traditional rules are designed around situations people expect in advance. AI decisioning works differently. Instead of waiting for a predefined condition, it can assess multiple signals, recognise changing patterns and recommend or take the next action based on the campaign objective.

This matters because a modern campaign can run across websites, mobile, connected TV, audio and other digital environments at the same time. What works in one market, format or moment may not work in another.

The scale of this change is significant. In its January 2026 outlook, the [IAB](https://www.iab.com/insights/2026-outlook/) found that **two-thirds of buyers were focused on agentic AI for advertising buying and campaign execution**. The same study found that five of the six areas where advertisers planned to increase focus were directly connected to AI.

## Why the Change Matters Now

The biggest advantage of AI decisioning is not simply speed. It is the ability to make decisions continuously.

Consider a campaign targeting several countries. A fixed system may apply the same instructions across every market. An AI-led approach can assess what is happening across different audiences and environments, then adjust its recommendations as conditions change.

That becomes increasingly important as advertising budgets grow more complex. WARC’s research found that **59% of marketers are concerned about AI disruption**, while **76% already use AI to summarise large amounts of information**.

The wider shift towards AI-led decision-making is also happening beyond advertising. Research from WARC estimates that **more than $944 billion in global consumer spending will be facilitated by AI agents in 2026**, showing how quickly AI is becoming part of everyday decision-making.

## AI Does Not Mean Removing People

Replacing fixed rules does not mean removing human judgement. The stronger model is collaboration. People define the business objective, brand boundaries and desired outcomes. AI can then help process information, identify opportunities and adjust decisions at a scale that would be difficult to manage manually.

That distinction is important as adoption grows. AI should make decision-making more responsive without making it less understandable or accountable.
